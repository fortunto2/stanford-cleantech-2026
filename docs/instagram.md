The smartest validation at a Stanford climate hackathon: rotate the wind 180° and watch the signal vanish.

Stanford Cleantech Hackathon, 23-24 Oct. 19 projects in 24 hours. I read every page and recorded the pitches. What people actually built:

• FlareWise: NASA fire pixels clustered with DBSCAN, a Gaussian plume on ERA5 wind, and a budget plan for which gas flares to capture. Pollution at the monitor rose downwind, and the signal vanished when they flipped the wind.
• The Chill Factor: weather stations are 50 km from the orchard. They validated by switching stations off and predicting them.
• Griddy: two chained XGBoost models score 16,393 grid cells across California, and the app flags its own flattered AUC.
• GridLight: 1 pm and 4 am cost the same on a PG&E plan, at 141 vs 324 g/kWh of CO2.
• Dengai: drone-found mosquito sites become team routes in OR-Tools. No LLM, one laptop.

What the investor panel said:
• Money follows data-center load. Hyperscalers buy transformers directly and want them fast.
• White space: adaptation, insurance, asset owners.
• Red flags: "we sell to utilities", subsidy as the business, AI where physics would do.

What I took away: everyone used the same free public data. The scarce input is labels and utility data, not models. LLMs read the PDFs; physics stays deterministic.
