⚙️ Hackathon Tracks
We’re excited to present three hackathon tracks for the event.


⚡Track 1: Energy & the Grid
Judging led by Silicon Valley Clean Energy
How can customers and the grid use cleaner, cheaper energy at the right time?
   1. Making time-of-use rates make sense. Most customers pay different prices at different times of day but have little sense of when to use energy or what an action will cost. Design a product, physical or virtual, that helps people understand their rates and consumption from a cost and carbon standpoint, and know when the good and bad times are to act.
Source: Silicon Valley Clean Energy 
   2. Open build. Bring your own solution to the track question, from smarter energy use at home to better ways of balancing supply and demand on the grid.
🌎Track 2: Climate & Health
Judging led by TotalEnergies and Stanford's Sustainability Accelerator
 How can we turn climate and environmental data into public health action that saves lives?
      1. Heat action plan audit. Build an LLM pipeline that finds US state and metro heat and cold plans, codes what they commit to, and scores them against the evidence of what actually works.
      2. AI route planning for dengue surveillance. Turn predicted mosquito breeding sites into cost-effective inspection routes for public health teams working under real limits on staff, time, and budget.
🔋Track 3: Resilient Systems
Judging led by SE Ventures
 How can we keep power on and communities safe as climate hazards intensify?
      1. How AI agents can re-invent sustainable home automation. Apple has just announced a new Home Automation strategy. Google and Tesla are also creating connected ecosystems. How to create a more efficient, decarbonized, and secure home that will leverage the full capacity of AI?
Source: SE Ventures 
      2. Predictive power outage and reliability mapping. Combine outage records, weather, and infrastructure data to predict where outages will hit hardest, then recommend distributed energy resources for the most exposed users.
Source: Rajagopal, Majumdar & Cezar, "ReliAdapt"

❗ Rules
In order to be eligible to win a prize, your team and project must follow all of the guidelines of Cleantech Hackathon:
               1. All members of your team must have checked-in to the Cleantech Hackathon  and have your Hackathon badge
               2. at least one member of your team must be physically present to demo your project to judges on Saturday (check schedule for specific time)
               3. all building must have taken place during hacking hours (5pm on Friday, Oct. 23 - 5pm on Saturday Oct. 24).
               1. Note: ideation and planning is allowed (and encouraged) before the hackathon, but all coding/building must take place during hacking hours.

🏆 Judging & Criteria
In order to be judged, you'll need to submit your project on Devpost. You need to create an account on Devpost and join ‘Stanford Cleantech Hackathon’ to submit your project.
On the Devpost, you give all the information about your project, including your team and the tracks you'd like to be considered for, including any challenges you might have hacked in.


In order to win a prize, you must be physically present during judging to demo your project to the judges.


Judging will take place in two rounds, the first will be reviewed on Devpost by the judges. Then 3 finalists per track will be chosen to present. The finalists will have 3 minutes presentation + 2 minutes questions (5 minute max) to present, so be ready to work through your demo quickly. Judges will then decide on the winner for each track.
