Line crews after a fire: a reading, not a wait

A line is down behind a fire front. The rule wants the employer to determine the AQI before the crew goes in and to log it; the smoke model has 40 km cells and the nearest monitor can be 48 km away. The dispatcher either waits for a canyon the wind has already cleared, or sends people without a record.

1 · Trigger

VIIRS sees the fire; the API issues a task with a bearing and a distance (dispatch).

2 · Reading

A sensor node, or a drone outside the flight restriction, samples PM2.5 at the work site and reports it; a photo of the sky is the second check.

3 · Record

The plan prints with the reading, the hour, the rule and the N95 line, signed at the briefing: the document the crew goes in with, and the one an inspector asks for.

Who pays: the utility or its contractor, per site, for the reading and the record. Hours saved per outage is the pilot’s number, not ours yet · §5141.1 requires N95s at AQI 151+, respirators above 500; it does not forbid the work
