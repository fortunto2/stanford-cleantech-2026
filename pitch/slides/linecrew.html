Line crews after a fire: a reading, not a wait

A line is down behind a fire front. The rule wants the air determined and logged before the crew goes in; the smoke model has 40 km cells and the nearest monitor can be 48 km away. The dispatcher waits for a canyon the wind has already cleared, or sends people without a record.

1 · Trigger

VIIRS sees the fire within 50 km; the API issues a task with a bearing and a distance (dispatch).

2 · Reading

A sensor node, or a drone outside the flight restriction, samples PM2.5 at the work site and reports it; a photo of the sky is the second check.

3 · Record

The plan prints with the reading, the hour, the rule and the N95 line, signed at the briefing: the document the crew goes in with, and the one an inspector asks for.

Who pays: the employer of the crew, per site, for the reading and the record; the utility is the channel and the pilot · Hours saved per outage is the pilot’s number, not ours yet · §5141.1 requires N95s at AQI 151+, respirators above 500; it does not forbid the work · Drone payload: a concept, the API side is live

The same product for a utility’s line crews. A line is down behind a fire. The rule says determine the air and log it before the crew goes in; the model is coarse and the monitor is far, so the dispatcher waits for a canyon the wind has already cleared, or sends people without a record. Our API hands out the task when the satellite sees the fire, a node or a drone takes the reading outside the flight restriction, and the plan prints with the reading and the rule as the record the crew goes in with. We do not claim hours saved; that is the pilot’s number. The payer is the employer of the crew, per site; the utility is the channel, and we sit inside a decision the dispatcher already makes: the record of how the air was determined is the insurance for that decision.
