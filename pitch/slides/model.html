Two of us. Back home in Sterlitamak, an industrial city in Russia, I organised residents to report bad air with evidence, and that is what got the agencies to act; then I built Air Signal, a live air map with ten thousand community sensors. The engine is rules and physics, the EPA NowCast formula, no language model in the loop; the only machine learning is the sky check. Adaptation, not mitigation: the fire is coming either way. The model. Everything a worker needs is free and open source: the engine, the rulebooks, the worker app, distributed through the people workers already trust. Employers pay per site for what they actually need to run a business: alerts in Spanish and English, many sites in one place, and the archive of plans and handled reports that an inspector or an insurer asks for, plus a feed for building systems. Raw reports stay private. What we publish is aggregate: exposure and report counts by county, anonymized, for researchers and agencies. SuperDuper is set up as a public-benefit company, so open data is part of the mission, not a giveaway.
